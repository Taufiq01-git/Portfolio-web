Tasmi’ - Every recitation, on record

A mobile-first web app for a Qur'an recitation program, with two sides. On the participant side, people register for two weekly sessions and log each recitation. On the supervisor side, supervisors verify, correct and report on them.A mobile-first web app for a Qur’an recitation program, with two sides. On the participant side, people register and log each recitation. On the supervisor side, supervisors verify and correct them.

It started in WhatsApp and Google Sheets. The quick first app that replaced them held 54 participants’ records by Batch 5, and lost people’s history three times. More patches would not fix that, so I first defined what every report may and may not do, then redesigned each screen around those rules.

ONE OF THE GAP · S.1 OF 30Three login rules, each correct alone, that made login impossible together.
THE PLAN · THREE RULES THAT EACH PASSED REVIEW
Log in with just a PINSo the list of participant names stays hidden.Hides the name list.
Store the PIN hashedSo a leaked database reveals no PINs.No PIN leaks.
Lock after wrong attemptsCounted on each participant’s own row.Counted per row.
THE GAP · WHY THEY BREAK TOGETHER
With only a PIN, the app must check every participant’s hash one by one: about 20 seconds per login. And since a guesser never names anyone, no row’s counter rises, so the lock never fires.
THE FIX · CLOSED BEFORE BUILD
A keyed lookup column finds the owner in one search. A unique index blocks shared PINs. Wrong attempts count per device.
HOW THE 30 STAY CLOSED

Four rules, read at the start of every session.

I built v1 with an AI coding assistant that forgets everything between sessions. The rules are what carries over.

ONE RULE FOR GOING BACK

Looking does not delete. Changing does.

Going back to check your group keeps your sessions. They clear only when the group or class actually changes, and the warning sits inline on that step, instead of a dialog that warned about a loss that had not happened yet.

V0A one-row fix meant fighting a table. Nine columns, one row per session. On a phone only three fit, so the supervisor scrolls sideways for level, pages and notes, and down to find the row. Save commits the whole table at once.
V1Fix one row, safely. A correction touches only that row. The old value stays, with who changed it and when, and the way back sits beside it.
REJECTEDBase history: every record carries the same weightSessions, absences and waiting periods share one card style, so the record to correct does not stand out.
REJECTEDWeekly timeline: too plain to edit fromRecords read as a log. Nothing shows a row can be tapped to correct it, and low contrast flattens the hierarchy.
REJECTEDMonth calendar: dates are easy to find, records are notDots say something happened, not what. Reaching a record takes an extra tap, and a day with two records hides one.
REJECTEDDense list: density hides the tap targetFitting more rows made each one smaller and lighter. Nothing signals a row opens for editing, and the hierarchy is unclear.
REJECTEDFill the page numbers in automaticallyAn invented number would stay in someone’s history for good, and a partly invented history cannot be trusted.
REJECTEDLeave Present out of the correctionsA wrong absence could only become Sick. The debt disappears, but the session that really happened is never recorded.
