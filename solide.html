Solide - One canvas, no second app

A design tool I built for myself first: layout, vector drawing and motion on one canvas, with my own AI working inside the file.

I worked across three apps and paid for two AI plans, and the only check that a built screen matched its design was my own eyes. The first Solide took one week on GrapesJS and then hit a wall, so I started over with the flows and the UI system first. Three months on, I have drawn more than 300 Tasmi' screens on it.

01 · USERS & CONSTRAINTS

One user to start: me, designing every screen and then building it.

Solide was built for its maker first. That made the brief unusually honest: every gap between a drawing and its code cost my own time, and every extra tool cost my own money.

02 · PROBLEM

Three tools, two AI bills, and a gap only my eyes were checking.

A normal day meant laying out screens in one app, drawing icons in another, and paying for the AI that plugs into each. Every switch cost a little focus, and every plan a little money.

Then came the handoff. Porting Solide's own sidebar from its mockup took four rounds of me spotting mismatches by eye. When I finally automated the check, its first run on a section I had already reviewed three times found 134 mismatches. My eyes were the only test, and they were losing.

PROBLEM STATEMENT

How might I draw layout, vectors and motion in one place, let my own AI work inside the file, and know, not hope, that the built screen matches?

03 · APPROACH

I threw away a working week and started with the flows and the UI system.

Solide began in late June 2026 as a one-week GrapesJS build: 84 commits that worked, until an audit found one 1,179-line engine file changed 52 times in 30 days.

On July 3 I started over. This time the framing, the flows and the UI system came first, and the code came after. Three rules have carried every session since.

06 · DESIGN SYSTEM

The UI system came first, and tokens get a screen of their own.

Solide's interface is built from its own tokens. They are edited on a dedicated screen, in light and dark, with math, contrast pairs and alias chains in view instead of buried in a panel.

07 · PROCESS AND PROOF

Every behaviour is drawn and signed off before its code.

The pen drew corners only. A curve could arrive in an SVG file, but dragging one of its points bent it out of shape, and a circle showed 5 points instead of 4.

So curves became 24 behaviours, A to X, each one drawn on the canvas and approved over four review rounds before I wrote a line of code. Six of them below.

If I can't measure it, I don't claim it.

Solide's own interface plays by the same rule. Its panels are split into 126 sections, and each one must pass the design-diff gate against its mockup before a commit goes through. The gate proves the DOM matches the drawing. It does not prove behaviour or accessibility.

08 · OUTCOME & REFLECTION

Solide already carries real work: more than 300 Tasmi' screens.

Every Tasmi' screen, from rough lo-fi to final hi-fi, was drawn on the Solide canvas and moved into code through its MCP. For a tool built for its maker, that is the test that counts: I kept using it.
